# Matched real-CXL before/after check — September 23, 2026

The earlier 3.728 GB/s local number was measured with **one publisher thread per broker route**. The historical local launcher (`scripts/lib/run_throughput_impl.sh`) used **four**; the paper's Fig. 1 launcher (`PaperScripts/run_fig1_throughput_scaling.sh`) defaults to **six**. On the same current broker binary, changing the local trial to four threads raised a qualifying 10 GiB ORDER5/ACK1/RF0 run to **10.912 decimal GB/s**. The thread-count mismatch is the established cause of the apparent roughly threefold local performance gap. It does not, by itself, explain every old/new implementation difference or reproduce the two-remote-publisher paper cell.

For the controlled broker comparison, we alternated the retained pre-refactor broker (`ae9959dd`, SHA256 `19455cc1becd5f95157f493acda5114709189222160a2542a1517aea7f5423a5`) and current refactored broker (`0f9c8f78`, SHA256 `109f49ba32396d6b9596447716ead47f16cb5c66237f4ab037bae286a2f0128c`) in three adjacent AB/BA pairs. Both used the *same* current client binary (SHA256 `edc4fcc931e1f2c01509f5dfc4e8c8acb0b5d31a4797b348a36302ee9e94e38e`), one local client on NUMA node 0, four brokers on node 1, a 64 GiB region resident on real CXL node 2, four threads per broker route, 10 GiB of 4 KiB messages, ORDER5/ACK1/RF0, and 2 MiB batches. All six trials passed exact 2,621,440-message ACK and four-broker routing checks, exited normally, and cleaned up their private shared-memory region. The retained old broker predates `--print-layout`; its wrapper used the current layout calculator for preflight, while the runtime still checked its required four segments and NUMA placement.

A separate *full-stack* comparison rebuilt both old broker and old client, then alternated old/old with current/current. Its first old/old trial passed at **11.149 GB/s**. The first current/current trial **failed validation**, so the campaign stopped and no valid full-stack performance estimate exists. In that failed run, broker 0 stalled at expected batch sequence 419 with 512 later batches held, then fenced the session after its 30-second lease. The client reopened and resent 513 suffix batches; its ACK frontier ultimately reported **2,626,344** messages for a target of **2,621,440**, an excess of **4,904**. Before the fixes below, `Publisher::Poll()` accepted any frontier `>= target` and the client exited zero even though the external exact-ACK validator rejected it. The failure and broker/client logs are preserved at `/home/domin/Embarcadero/results/cxl-before-after/embarcadero-dev-1002-vcjeb1uc/`; the stopped campaign index is `paired-full-stack-threads4-index.json`.

The ACK overcount has a code-level root cause. Broker 0 sends `Topic::GetClientOrdered(client_id)`, which accumulates committed messages **across** session epochs; sequencer recovery restores that cumulative count. The client's `SessionGlobalAckFromGeneration` nevertheless added its pre-fence `ack_message_base_` to every wire ACK after reopening. In this run the base was **211,176**. The observed synthetic frontier of 2,626,344 therefore corresponded to only **2,415,168** on the broker's cumulative frontier, still **206,272 short** of the target. The ACK normalization now takes the maximum of the local committed base and the *already cumulative* wire value; the ORDER5 `Poll` guard separately rejects any final frontier above target. The former prevents false credit; the latter makes unexpected over-ack fail closed. The `SessionGlobalAckFromWire` unit test covers post-fence, replayed, and stale wire values.

The initial batch-419 stall is only localized, not yet attributed to a specific first trigger. `QueueBuffer::SealCurrentAndAdvance` numbers each batch before putting it on one of 16 independent sender queues; `Publisher::PublishThread` sends each queue on a separate TCP connection. `NetworkManager::HandlePublishRequest` receives the header and payload, reserves a BLog slot, and publishes its PBR entry. `Topic::BrokerScannerWorker5` moves ready PBR entries into an epoch buffer. The ORDER5 classifier commits only `seq == next_expected` and holds later batches. The retained log proves that the committed prefix stopped at 418, the hold reached its 512-entry limit, and the 30-second lease fenced the session. It has no per-batch client-send, broker-receive, PBR-publish, scanner, or classifier trace for 419, so it cannot distinguish a slow sender/socket from an ingress or broker-stage loss. The experiment runner set a **60-second minimum retransmit timeout**, greater than the **30-second session lease**, so a retained missing predecessor could not be retried before the fence. The configured 512 MiB client-pool maximum was also ineffective: `QueueBuffer::AddBuffers` requires `16 × 32 + 1 = 513` slots and allocated **1,075,904,640 bytes** of 2 MiB slots. That approximately matches the 512-entry hold window and turns one unresolved predecessor into full retention/backpressure. These settings amplify a gap; they do not establish its first trigger.

To verify the recovery/ACK path independently, an owned real-CXL diagnostic deliberately delayed batch 40 by 7 seconds with a 5-second lease. A 256 MiB indexed run fenced at committed batch 39, reopened with 91 retained suffix batches, and then passed **exactly 65,536 ACKs** and an ordered subscriber audit of **65,536/65,536 messages**, zero duplicates, parse errors, or export gaps. It exited normally with all four brokers resident on node 2 and cleaned up its private mapping. The manifest and logs are in `/home/domin/Embarcadero/results/cxl-before-after/embarcadero-dev-1002-sgzcccfe/`. This proves the corrected cumulative ACK handling on one real rollover; it does not reproduce the unknown first trigger in the original 10 GiB failure.

Follow-up with the corrected client completed a fresh **10 GiB**, four-thread local real-CXL transfer at exact 2,621,440 ACKs, zero retransmissions/fences, and all four routes active (`embarcadero-dev-1002-idhjcgfi`, client SHA256 `47cdc99f6f7ab500d8788a44546f6f10b6378c1d0a75cfeb086e9867323f6ef1`). This is one correctness pass, not a frequency estimate for the original intermittent gap. `QueueBuffer::AddBuffers` was then corrected to treat the configured pool maximum as a hard limit: under 512 MiB it now allocates **255** 2 MiB-plus-header slots (534,806,400 bytes), instead of overriding the cap with its 513-slot pipeline target. The owned development runner's retry floor is now **10 seconds**, inside the broker's default 30-second lease; benchmark profiles that explicitly retain 60 seconds remain separately identified. Two new 10 GiB runs with the 255-slot pool and 10-second floor also completed exact ACKs with zero retries/fences, all four routes, node-2 placement and owned cleanup. The first (`embarcadero-dev-1002-7d__4z67`) was rejected by an obsolete test oracle that hardcoded `session_rto_min_ms=60000`; its client and broker completed normally. The oracle now checks each profile's configured value, and the repeat (`embarcadero-dev-1002-vryf1odp`, client SHA256 `7f9fd5074296c62914bff8f78b688e52ac05385ea2eddefd8c2d851046e3e786`) passed. The observed ACK throughput of these differently configured single runs is not a before/after performance comparison. Neither normal run reproduces the original batch-419 stall.

A larger forced-gap test exposed another real recovery defect under the hard pool limit. With a 1 GiB indexed workload and only 255 pool slots, batch 40 was delayed for 7 seconds and the broker fenced after 5. The client requeued **254** batches and then stopped at **261,640/262,144 ACKs**, exactly one full 504-message batch short. The original fence drain target used `Publisher::client_order_`, which the caller increments only after `QueueBuffer::Write()` returns. When that call seals and enqueues a full batch but blocks acquiring its next slot, `client_order_` is one batch behind the actual sender queue. `WaitForSessionSendDrain()` could therefore return before that last batch had been sent and entered the retained unacked list; the fence snapshot omitted it. The first test also exhausted its separate 256 MiB subscriber-retention setting, so it could not provide an end-to-end payload audit (`embarcadero-dev-1002-se9ah2r9`). QueueBuffer now publishes its own unique-message count at successful queue insertion; the fence waits for the larger of that count and the caller count, and fails closed if the drain times out. Repeating the same 1 GiB real-CXL delay with a 2 GiB subscriber-retention allowance requeued **255** suffix batches, passed **262,144/262,144 exact ACKs** and an indexed delivery audit of all **262,144 messages** with zero duplicates, parse errors or export gaps (`embarcadero-dev-1002-wv5r93ma`). This explains the pressure-test shortfall, but the first trigger of the earlier *natural* batch-419 gap is still unobserved.

The one-thread broker-only paired trials were also close (old median **3.750**, current median **3.694 GB/s**), but varied more widely and are less representative of the historical local launcher. The prior two-remote-publisher result of **8.874 GB/s** likewise used one thread per broker route versus the paper launcher's six; it is not a matched comparison to the paper's **11.130 GB/s** bar. A paper-performance claim requires a matched six-thread remote rerun with the appropriate remote hosts and network settings, a frozen full-stack candidate, and repeatable full-stack correctness. The cause and frequency of the original batch-419 stall remain unmeasured.

## Epoch-seal race investigation, September 24

A fresh code-path review found a concrete loss window in the broker's ORDER5 epoch buffer. A scanner first marked its broker active and then rechecked `COLLECTING`, while `seal()` published `SEALED` before waiting for active scanners. The sequencer and recovery code could observe that provisional `SEALED`, drain the buffer, and reset it while the admitted scanner was still about to enqueue a batch. A late enqueue into the drained buffer would then be erased by the next reset. The scanner-to-sequencer ownership invariant was reproduced with a concurrent epoch-buffer test: it failed against the old implementation and passed after the fix. Commit `f754c906` serializes scanner admission with the transition to a new `SEALING` state and publishes `SEALED` only after admitted scanners have exited. The full configured test suite passed (54/54). This race also exists in the retained pre-refactor code; it is not evidence of a regression introduced by the recent refactor.

The natural 10 GiB gap remains an inference rather than a per-batch proof of that race. In one old-broker run (`embarcadero-dev-1002-3zhlphl4`), batch **4743** was retained by the client after send and was still missing at the broker's 30-second fence. Aggregate scanner-push counts reached **5,202**, the complete original batch count, while the classifier committed **4,743** and held **458** successors. Those counts make loss after scanner push plausible, but there is no per-sequence scanner/epoch record to establish where batch 4743 went. The client recovered to exactly **2,621,440 ACKs** after the fence; the strict zero-fence run failed. A separate initial attempt (`embarcadero-dev-1002-zjh2_u8_`) completed its transfer but was rejected because a symlinked client path did not satisfy the harness's executable-identity check; it was not used as a benchmark sample.

We compared the old broker from `e0fe9cc6` with the fixed broker from `f754c906`, holding the diagnostic client binary constant. Three adjacent AB/BA pairs used the same four-broker real-CXL local profile, 513-slot pool, 60-second RTO, NUMA binding, and exact-ACK/zero-fence validator. The per-trial manifest index is `/home/domin/Embarcadero/results/cxl-before-after/paired-epoch-seal-index.json`; its binary hashes and observed `atomic128` PBR mode match within each version. An extra old-broker attempt before these pairs reproduced the natural fence and is recorded in `paired-epoch-seal-first-repro.json`, separately from qualified throughput samples.

The current evidence supports fixing the demonstrated race first; kernel tracing would only be justified if gaps continue and lighter per-sequence tracing cannot locate their stage.
